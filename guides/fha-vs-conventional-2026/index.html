FHA vs conventional in 2026

Both can work with a small down payment. The difference is mostly in mortgage insurance: what it costs and whether you can ever get rid of it.

Down payment and credit

Conventional loans start at 3% down for some first-time buyers and generally need a 620+ score. FHA needs 3.5% down with a 580+ score, or 10% down from 500 to 579.

Mortgage insurance

FHA charges 1.75% upfront plus an annual premium, most often 0.55% for a 30-year loan with under 5% down, and it lasts for the life of the loan unless you put at least 10% down (then 11 years). Conventional PMI varies with credit and down payment and can be cancelled at 80% LTV.

Loan limits

The 2026 conforming limit is $832,750 for most counties. FHA limits vary by county, starting at about $541,287 in lower-cost areas.

Which wins?

With a score above about 700 and 5% or more down, conventional is usually cheaper. With lower scores, FHA's flat pricing often wins, at least until you can refinance. Compare with your numbers.
