How to remove PMI

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender, not you. Here is every way to stop paying it, in the order most people can use them.

1. Ask at 80% of the original value

The Homeowners Protection Act lets you request cancellation once your principal balance reaches 80% of the original value of the home (the lower of the purchase price or appraisal). You need to be current, have a good payment history, and may need to show the home hasn't lost value. Put the request in writing.

2. Get there faster with extra payments

Extra principal payments move the 80% date forward. They don't move the automatic 78% date, so you have to ask. Use the PMI removal calculator to find how much extra a month reaches 80% by the date you want.

3. Use a new appraisal if your home gained value

Many servicers will cancel PMI based on current value: commonly 75% LTV after two years of payments or 80% after five. Ask your servicer for its rules before ordering an appraisal.

4. Automatic termination at 78%

If you do nothing, PMI must end when your balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the original value, provided you're current. It must also end at the loan's midpoint regardless.

5. Refinance

Refinancing into a new conventional loan at 80% LTV or less removes PMI, and is the usual way to remove FHA MIP. Weigh the closing costs with the refinance calculator.
