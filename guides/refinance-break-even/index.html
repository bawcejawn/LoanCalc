Refinance break-even, explained properly

The quick formula is a good first look. Here is what it leaves out and how to tell whether a refinance really pays.

The quick formula

Divide total closing costs by the drop in monthly payment. $6,000 in costs and $250 a month in savings is a 24-month break-even.

What it misses

A lower payment on a fresh 30-year loan partly comes from stretching the debt over more years. Early payments on the new loan are mostly interest, so your balance may fall more slowly than it would have. A fair comparison counts the cash you keep each month, subtracts what you paid to refinance, and subtracts any extra balance you still owe compared with keeping your old loan.

Making a refinance pay

  • Plan to stay well past the true break-even.
  • Keep paying your old payment amount on the new loan.
  • Compare a shorter term at a lower rate.
  • Ask for lender credits if you may move soon.
