The three ways conventional PMI ends

Under the Homeowners Protection Act, you can ask your servicer to cancel PMI once your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, either on schedule or sooner through extra payments. PMI must end automatically when the balance is scheduled to reach 78%, and no later than the midpoint of the loan term, as long as you're current on payments.

The automatic date is based only on the original schedule. Extra payments do not move it, so if you prepay you need to ask. That gap is often worth hundreds or thousands of dollars.

Removal based on a new appraisal

If your home has gained value, many servicers will cancel PMI based on a new appraisal. A common standard (Fannie Mae's) is 75% loan-to-value of the current value after two to five years of payments, or 80% after five years. Enter today's value to see whether this route is faster for you.

FHA, VA and USDA

FHA mortgage insurance can't be cancelled by request: it lasts 11 years with at least 10% down, otherwise for the life of the loan. USDA's annual fee lasts for the life of the loan. VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance. For FHA and USDA, refinancing into a conventional loan at 80% LTV or below is the usual way out.

Questions

What do I need to request PMI cancellation?

Usually a written request, a good payment history (no 30-day late payments in the last year), and evidence the home has not lost value, which may mean an appraisal.

Does an appraisal count toward the 80%?

For the federal 80% and 78% rules, value means the original purchase price or appraisal. Removal based on a new, higher value is a servicer or investor policy, not a federal right.

Is PMI tax deductible?

It depends on current tax law and your situation. Ask a tax professional.
