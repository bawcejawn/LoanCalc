Refinance break-even calculator

A true break-even that counts closing costs, points and the extra years a new loan adds, not just the change in monthly payment.

Why the simple break-even misleads

The usual shortcut divides closing costs by monthly savings. It ignores that a new 30-year loan restarts the clock, so more of each payment goes to interest and you may owe more for longer. This calculator tracks both loans month by month and counts you ahead only when the cash you've kept, minus costs, outweighs any extra balance you still owe.

When refinancing tends to pay

Refinancing usually makes sense when you can lower your rate meaningfully, you'll stay past the break-even point, and you don't restart a long term without a plan. Keeping your old payment amount on the new loan is a simple way to get the lower rate without stretching the debt.

Questions

How much does it cost to refinance?

Typically 2% to 5% of the loan amount for lender, appraisal and title fees. Your Loan Estimate lists them.

Should I roll closing costs into the loan?

It saves cash today but you pay interest on those costs. The calculator shows both options.

Are points worth it?

A point costs 1% of the loan and usually lowers the rate by about a quarter point. It pays off only if you keep the loan long enough.
