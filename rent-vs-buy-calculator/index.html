Should I rent or buy?

Compare renting and buying the same kind of home, and see which leaves you with more money after the years you'd stay.

How the comparison works

Buying costs more up front (down payment and closing costs) and comes with bills renters don't pay: property tax, insurance, upkeep and selling costs later. Renting costs rent, which usually rises every year. To compare fairly, the calculator gives both paths the same money: the renter invests the cash a buyer would spend up front, and each month whoever pays less invests the difference. At the end it compares what each would have.

What matters most

How long you stay matters most. Buying and selling cost a lot, so short stays often favor renting. After that come the gap between rent and the full cost of owning, how fast home prices and rents grow, and what your savings could earn.

Questions

Is it always better to buy?

No. If you might move within a few years, or rent is cheap compared with home prices where you live, renting can leave you with more money.

Does this include tax deductions?

No. Most homeowners now take the standard deduction, so mortgage interest often doesn't lower their taxes.

What upkeep should I expect?

A common rule of thumb is about 1% of the home's value each year, more for older homes.
